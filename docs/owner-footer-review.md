## The footer

The footer is the bottom of every page. You build it on the **Footer** screen without writing any HTML.

- **Contact column.** Tick "Show a Contact column" and the footer shows the phone number and email address from Settings, under a "Contact" heading. Change them in Settings and the footer follows. If neither is set, the column links to your contact page instead.
- **Link groups.** Each group has a heading (for example "More" or "Our services") and a list of links. Each link has its text and an address, such as `/about/` or `https://example.com/`. Use "Add link", "Move up", "Move down" and "Remove" inside a group, and "Add a link group", "Move group up", "Move group down" and "Remove group" for the groups themselves. Every one of those buttons saves the footer straight away and tells you what changed. You can have up to 6 groups and 20 links in each.
- **Small print.** One line of plain text under the columns, for example a registration or licence number. The copyright line with the year and your name is added on its own; you don't need to type it.

With Spanish pages turned on, the screen has an "English footer" and a "Spanish footer" tab. Spanish pages use the Spanish footer when it has something in it, and the English footer otherwise.

Links from one language to the other (for example "En español" pointing at `/es/`) are marked with their language automatically, so screen readers switch voice.

If your site was set up before the Footer screen existed, it keeps using the footer HTML from the Site template screen until you turn on the Contact column or add a group. The Footer screen tells you when that is the case.

## Scheduling a page

A page can be published ahead of time. On the page's edit screen, set its status to Published and fill in **Publish on** with a date and time. Until that moment visitors get "Page not found" and the Pages list shows the page as **Scheduled**; you can still open it yourself while signed in, which is how you check it. When the time comes it simply appears; nothing else to do. Leave "Publish on" empty to publish straight away. A page you have scheduled is not added to the menu on its own: add it from the Menu screen when you want the link to show.

To see a draft or scheduled page as visitors will, press "Save and view page" while signed in.

## Review links

A review link lets someone see one page or post exactly as visitors will, without signing in, even while it is a draft or scheduled. Use it to show a colleague or client a page before it goes live.

1. Open the page or post in the control panel and go to the **Review links** heading at the end of the screen.
2. Choose how long the link should work (1 to 30 days; 7 is the usual) and press "Create a review link".
3. The new link appears in a box under Review links. Put your cursor in the box, select all and copy, then paste it into an email or chat.

Whoever opens the link sees the page with a yellow banner at the top: "Review copy of a draft, shared by (your name) until (date)". Search engines are told to stay away from it. When the time is up, or when you press **Revoke** next to the link, the address shows "This review link has expired" with a link to your contact page. Deleting a page or post removes its review links too.

Anyone who has the link can open it, so treat it like the page itself: don't share it more widely than you would the draft.
